Google Entity Links: The Fastest-Indexing Backlink Type You're Not Using

5 July 2026 · 2 min read

Every SEO has experienced the frustration of a freshly built backlink sitting uncrawled for days or weeks. There's a specific link category that mostly sidesteps this problem: links built through Google's own trusted properties — Sites, Docs, Blogger — which get crawled unusually fast precisely because Google prioritizes its own infrastructure.

Why speed compounds with everything else you're doing

A campaign that shows crawl and trust signal within days, rather than sitting invisible for weeks while a slower campaign matures, keeps momentum visible in your reporting and gives Google an early, positive signal about your target page. A Google entity link package is specifically built around this speed advantage.

What this tactic doesn't provide on its own

Fast indexing isn't the same as strong ranking authority — entity links are a discovery and momentum accelerant, not a substitute for the deeper authority signals that move a competitive keyword. Treat the speed as a bonus layered on top of a real campaign, not the entire campaign.

The ideal sequencing for a new campaign

Order entity links at the very start of a broader push, running them alongside a slower-building contextual or authority campaign, so your target page shows activity immediately while the higher-value links mature over the following weeks.

A mistake to avoid: expecting entity links to carry a whole campaign

The value here is speed of indexing and early crawl signal, not deep ranking authority comparable to an editorial link. Buyers who order this instead of, rather than alongside, a genuine authority campaign tend to see fast indexing but a much lower ranking ceiling.

What to expect after ordering

Expect placements to go live within a few days, with crawl signal typically visible in Search Console within 48 to 72 hours given Google's fast indexing of its own properties. Pair this with a slower-building campaign and track them together rather than expecting this listing alone to move rankings independently.

Frequently asked: is this the same as regular Web 2.0 backlinks?

Not quite — while both involve building content on platforms you don't own outright, entity links specifically use Google's own first-party properties (Sites, Docs, Drive, Blogger) rather than general third-party Web 2.0 platforms, which is what gives them their distinctive fast-indexing advantage. A general Web 2.0 package draws from a wider range of platforms and focuses more on durability and content variety than on the specific crawl-speed benefit this listing is built around.

The bottom line: speed of discovery is an underrated lever in SEO, and Google's own properties offer a shortcut most campaigns never use deliberately. Layer this in at the start of any new push to keep momentum visible while your slower, higher-authority links mature in the background.
