Anchor Text Ratios: The SEO Detail Most Campaigns Get Wrong

21 June 2026 · 2 min read

Anchor text is unusual among ranking signals in that it's entirely within the buyer's control — you choose exactly what text a link uses. That control is a double-edged sword: it's also the single easiest signal to overuse in a way that triggers algorithmic scrutiny, since a genuinely organic link profile never has 80% of its anchors using the exact same exact-match phrase.

What a natural distribution actually looks like

Real, organically-earned backlink profiles skew heavily toward branded and generic anchors — your company name, "click here," "this article" — with only a modest share of exact-match or partial-match keyword phrases. A strategic anchor text campaign is built around designing that ratio deliberately rather than leaving it to chance or defaulting to keyword-heavy anchors.

Why past over-optimization needs a corrective approach

Sites that ran aggressive exact-match campaigns in the past — common in older, more aggressive SEO eras — often need a rebalancing campaign specifically weighted toward branded and generic anchors to dilute an over-optimized ratio before it triggers a manual review or algorithmic filter.

Getting the foundation right from the first link

For a site entering a competitive keyword for the first time, building the anchor text ratio correctly from day one avoids needing a corrective campaign later — audit your current ratio before adding volume from a bulk backlink package, so new links reinforce a healthy distribution rather than compounding an existing imbalance.

A mistake to avoid: not auditing your existing ratio first

Ordering anchor-text-focused links without first checking your current distribution risks reinforcing an imbalance rather than correcting one. Pull your existing anchor text report from Ahrefs, SEMrush, or Search Console before the campaign begins, so the new links are built to correct or complement what's already there.

What results typically look like

Expect placement to unfold over several days as each link's anchor text is deliberately chosen. Because anchor text ratio issues often relate to algorithmic filtering rather than a simple ranking boost, results can show up as a site recovering lost visibility as much as a site gaining new rankings — track both.

Frequently asked: what's a "safe" percentage of exact-match anchor text?

There's no single universal number, since safe ratios vary by niche competitiveness and existing profile history, but as a general guideline most defensible profiles keep exact-match anchors under 10 to 15 percent of the total, with the majority split between branded and generic phrases. A proper strategy session reviews your specific existing profile and niche before recommending a ratio, rather than applying a one-size-fits-all percentage.

The bottom line: anchor text is one of the few ranking signals fully within your control, which makes it worth getting deliberately right rather than leaving to chance. A rebalanced, strategically distributed profile protects existing rankings and gives new campaigns a healthier foundation to build on.
