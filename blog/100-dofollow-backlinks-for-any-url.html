100 Dofollow Backlinks Explained: When Cheap Link Volume Actually Helps

20 August 2026 · 3 min read

If you've spent any time reading SEO advice, you've probably absorbed the idea that cheap, high-volume backlinks are useless at best and dangerous at worst. That reputation is partly earned — but it also misses a real, narrow use case where a package like 100 dofollow backlinks genuinely does its job: getting a brand-new URL noticed by Google faster than it would be on its own.

The problem volume links actually solve

A freshly published page has no link equity and no crawl history. Googlebot doesn't know it exists until something points at it or your sitemap gets crawled. A batch of dofollow links spread across blog comments, forum profiles, and Web 2.0 properties creates exactly that: a set of crawl paths into a page that otherwise sits invisible for days or weeks. This is a discovery and indexing problem, not a ranking problem, and it's the one thing this kind of package reliably fixes.

What it won't do

A hundred low-authority links will not out-rank a competitor with three guest posts on genuinely respected industry sites. If you're trying to move a competitive commercial keyword, volume links are a supporting layer at best — something to run alongside, not instead of, a higher-authority package. Treating a $3.99 link bundle as a complete strategy is the single most common reason buyers end up disappointed with cheap backlinks.

How to use volume links without wasting them

The pattern that works: order volume links early, on new or recently updated pages, specifically to accelerate discovery — then layer contextual or high-DA links on top over the following weeks as the actual ranking push. Anchor text should stay varied (branded and generic phrases, not exact-match keyword stuffing) since that's what the algorithm expects from organic growth, not manufactured link buying.

Common mistakes buyers make with volume links

The most frequent mistake is pointing every single order at the same URL, over and over, instead of spreading volume across multiple pages that each need a discovery boost. A close second is expecting a ranking jump within days — volume links accelerate crawling and indexing, and that effect shows up quickly, but the ranking benefit (if any) compounds slowly alongside everything else you're doing on the page.

What a realistic timeline looks like

Expect crawl and indexing improvements within the first one to two weeks — that's the part this package is actually built to deliver. Any ranking movement beyond that point depends on the rest of your SEO work, including on-page optimization and any higher-authority links running alongside it, so measure this listing by discovery speed, not by rank position alone.

Frequently asked: does link velocity matter for a package this size?

Yes, though less than buyers often assume. Google is more concerned with sudden, unnatural spikes than with steady growth, and a hundred links delivered over several days rather than in one instant burst reads as far more natural than the same volume landing all at once. If you're running several volume orders on the same URL over time, spacing them out by a few weeks each avoids creating the kind of spike pattern that draws unwanted algorithmic attention, even though each individual order is entirely legitimate on its own.

The bottom line: cheap volume links have a real, specific job — accelerating discovery on new or recently updated pages — and they do that job well when used for exactly that purpose. Judge this listing by how fast your new URLs get crawled and indexed, not by how much it moves a competitive keyword on its own, and it will consistently earn its place in a broader campaign.
